Cards: does every card work, for the engine and for the model?

Two checks. The engine must play all 26 kingdom cards correctly. The model must be able to handle every card and every question a card asks (which card to trash, whether to skip an action with Library, what to put back with Sentry, and so on).

The engine

The engine (engine/) has a hand-made position for every card in tests/test_rules.py. Market, Laboratory, Festival, Chapel, Moneylender and Mine got theirs in rl-v3, along with Throne Room on each of the 25 other action cards. tests/test_cards_all.py plays each card in 40 whole games between random players, in both the Python and the C engine. Each card must be bought and played, every question it asks must come up, and both engines must play identical games. The one exception is Bandit's choice between Silver and Gold, which only comes up when a victim reveals both; the parity test covers it. 83 engine tests pass.

The model

For each card: games against the GA genome on kingdoms with that card plus 9 random others (40 kingdoms × 2 seats). Its own choices: it buys what it wants. Takes it: the share of games in which it bought at least one. Plays: plays per game. Questions: the decisions this card asks and whether they came up. "Not asked (never played)" means the model never bought the card, so the question could not come up. That is a choice, not a bug. With 2 forced copies: the same games, but its first buys are forced to two copies. This shows whether it can play the card, and what it answers (for Chapel: which cards it trashes and how often it stops). Errors: illegal answers, which must be 0.

What we found, and the card school

rl-v1's champion never made an illegal choice, but it never bought Chapel, and it rarely bought Mine, Library, Moneylender, Council Room, Smithy, Village and Merchant. A network cannot learn to play a card it never owns, so it cannot find out whether buying the card would pay. rl-v3's curriculum therefore has a card school. In 15% of the training games the learner's first buys are forced to two copies of one of these cards. Those forced buys are not trained on: it learns to play the cards from real games, and whether to buy them stays its own decision.

Can it trash? Yes. The 0% for Chapel is "never bought", not "cannot". With two forced Chapels, rl-v1's champion plays Chapel about 4 times a game, but cautiously: it mostly stops after trashing one Copper, and sometimes trashes a Gold. After 300 iterations with the card school, rl-v3 trashes Estates 14 times as often, has stopped trashing Gold, and still often trashes the Chapel itself. Mine works for both: Copper into Silver, Silver into Gold.

Why "bought, never played" is not a bug either. When rl-v3 buys Chapel or Mine on its own (in about 2–5% of games), it is almost always in the last one to three turns of the game, with a spare buy. The game ends before the card is drawn. In the few games where it buys one early, it plays it (3–5 times a game).
