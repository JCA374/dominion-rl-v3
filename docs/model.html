The model

rl-v1's network, trained on: one small network answers every decision of the game, and since rl-v3 it also reads rl-v2's helper inputs. There is no search and no chain module. The planner we built to calculate chains is a measuring instrument, not part of the player (see Chains).

The network

 413 inputs = 298 (what I know + the question) + 115 helper inputs (exact odds)
   │  dense 413 → 256, ReLU
   │  dense 256 → 256, ReLU
   ├──▶ 34 logits: a card id (or a Sentry option) or PASS; illegal answers masked out → softmax → a choice
   └──▶ 1 number: tanh → my win estimate in [−1, +1]
 180,771 weights; one forward pass ≈ 25 µs in C; a whole game ≈ 3 ms

A chain of actions is a series of single decisions. "Which action now?" is asked again after every card, and each time the inputs show the new hand, the cards already in play and the actions left. To play Festival before Smithy, the network has to learn that, with one action left, a terminal card in hand while a +Actions card waits is worth less than the other way round. On the chain exam rl-v1's champion does this right 91.6% of the time.

Where it starts: rl-v1's checkpoint, widened

rl-v1 r2 stopped at iteration 2,219 while its score against the GA genome was still rising (67% at the start of r2, 81% at the end). rl-v3 loads that checkpoint: the weights and also Adam's running averages, so the optimiser goes on as if nothing had happened. The first layer then gets 115 new input columns with zero weights, and zero Adam moments for them. A zero-weight input changes nothing, so iteration 0 plays exactly like rl-v1. PPO then finds out for itself which helper inputs are worth listening to.

The helper inputs (from rl-v2, rl_helpers in c/rl.c), computed exactly from what the player knows:

  • 11 about the next hand: the expected coins, the chance of at least $3, $5, $6 and $8, the expected action cards, +Actions, +Buys and +Cards in it, and the expected victory points it can buy. The next hand is the top 5 of the draw pile (its contents are known, its order is not), with the rest from a reshuffle when fewer than 5 are left.
  • 5 about the whole deck, per 5 cards: coins, action cards, +Actions, +Buys, +Cards.
  • 99 about one more copy of each card: how the deck's coins, +Actions and +Buys per 5 cards would change if it bought one.

In rl-v2 these inputs lifted the from-zero run from 71% to 74% against the GA genome.

How it learns: PPO against a league

The games. 3,000 per iteration. The network samples its choices from its probabilities, so it tries things. The opponents come from the stage's league (Curriculum): its past snapshots, the GA genome, the three bots, az-v1's network and search and, from stage 2, rl-v1's frozen champion. Games against its current self train both seats.

The update. Every decision gets an advantage: how much better the game ended (+1 win, 0 shared, −1 loss) than its own win estimate expected at that moment (GAE, λ = 0.95). PPO makes decisions with a positive advantage more likely and negative ones less likely, by at most ±20% in probability ratio. It runs 3 passes, and stops early if the policy moves too far (KL > 0.03). A small entropy bonus (0.01) keeps it trying alternatives.

The card school. In stages 2 and 3, 15% of the games are on a kingdom with one of ten cards the champion rarely buys, and the learner's first buys (up to two copies, in its first six turns) are forced to that card. Those forced buys are not recorded, so PPO never sees them as the network's choices. Everything the network does with the card afterwards (playing it, trashing with it, building around it) is learned like any other decision.

The turn planner (measured, not used)

At an action decision the planner copies the game and hides what the player cannot see: the order of the draw pile and the opponent's hand (de_hide). For each legal option it plays that option, lets the network finish the turn, and scores the result. It does this on K shuffles; every option sees the same K shuffles, so the comparison is fair. There are two scores: the network's win estimate after the turn (the "value judge"), or the coins at the first buy (the "coins judge"). The engine gained de_resume_action to continue a copied game from inside the action phase. With either judge, the planner did not make the network win more games.
